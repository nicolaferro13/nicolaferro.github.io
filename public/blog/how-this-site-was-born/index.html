How this site was born

16 September 2026

A brief summary of the whys and hows behind the idea of my personal site

The need

The original idea for this site started the day I obtained my Master’s degree (on the 27th of March 2026). From there, I started updating my CV (with a certain degree of delay) and sending it out. I had a few interviews with local companies, but then summer came and the whole country slowed down (as always, especially in August). Having some spare time, I started to think of other ways to showcase my work. The idea of a site was obvious, but I had always believed it to be too complex and time-consuming to set up.

A day at the beach

The turning point came at the end of August on a day at the beach, like many others. I was chatting with my friend M. when he mentioned that he had just rebuilt his own personal website, so I brought up my issue that was troubling me and he offered a solution, as it was the most obvious thing ever: just implement a static website with Hugo and host it on Github pages. Easier said than done. The problem was that I knew absolutely nothing about web development: it had never interested me nor had I ever needed to study it (I’m an energy engineer, not an ICT professional), additionally I knew nothing about formatting: zero knowledge of HTML5, JS and CSS. And, guess what, Fortran is not exactly a good language for web design.

What to do

Since my laptop was away for maintenance, I began by using an old but workable desktop PC. I knew I wouldn’t be able to build a website completely from scratch, at least not within a reasonable timeframe, so I decided to start with a Hugo template. After testing three different options, I settled on E25DX, which best suited my needs. However, I still needed to customize it to make it my own.

Hands on

The first attempts were heavily plagued by the lack of knowledge regarding web design and the basic functioning of static websites. Additionally, I ran into serious issues in getting hugo server to work on my old desktop PC, which forced me to commit my project to GitHub an insane amount of times, just to view basic modifications (luckily with my laptop back development became much faster). The help of AI tools was fundamental to understand the basics and avoid dead ends, as my skills improved I started writing my own .css and .html files allowing me to get closer to the desired result.

What’s next

Initially I planned to set up the site with just an extended CV description (“About me” section) and personal projects. However, as I progressed, I decided to keep the “blog” section (which is native to the E25DX template) for miscellaneous articles and personal experiences and interests. The structure will be in constant evolution and new sections or features may suddenly appear. After all, the main insight I gained during this development is that a personal website is never really finished!
